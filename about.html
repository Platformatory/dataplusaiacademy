About the Academy

Learn by building. Not by watching MOOCs at 2x.

The Academy is run by Platformatory Labs, a forward deployed engineering company. The engineers who do that work for customers are the ones who teach you.

1

We started the Academy to solve a hiring problem of our own

Platformatory works with customers on problems at petabyte scale and single-digit-millisecond latency. Hiring for that work means looking for Forward Deployed Engineers: people who go to the customer, reason from first principles and ship. Very few of the people we interviewed could do that, whether they had just graduated or had been working for fifteen years.

Two gaps, again and again. The first is first-principles reasoning about data & AI systems: the ability to walk a request from the wire up through every abstraction it pretends isn't there. The second is engineering rigour and taste, the sense for when a design is right and not merely working.

These engineers had consumed hundreds of hours of courseware. They had never once been on call for it. We looked for courses that taught what was actually missing and found none, so we built our own.

2

Courses designed as real-life projects

Each course answers one of those gaps. The agentic track goes to the core of building multi-agent systems, well past anything that's a thin wrapper around an LLM. The data and AI engineering is taught together with the infrastructure it always rests on. And everything is immersed in real-life customer problems, because that is the actual job.

The first cohort was twelve people in a room with our own engineers. No slides, real clusters, code reviewed like it was going to production, because functionally it was. The formula was simple: sit next to people doing the real work until you can do it too.

That is now twelve-plus cohorts and ninety-plus engineers deep, many of whom work at Platformatory, and whose graduates mentor the next cohort. The loop closes on purpose.

3

Nominally priced

Teaching is not a revenue line for us. Training fees cover operations; the GPUs, infrastructure and engineer-hours come from the company's own stack.

If you would benefit from a course but can't afford the fees, contact us about a scholarship. Scholarships come with a Platformatory internship, which is why we can offer a full waiver or a deep discount. Interns work on our open-source projects and large-scale customer simulations, never on billable client work.

Enrolment and day-to-day operations are run with Acyra, our coordinator & training operations partner, which keeps our engineers doing what only they can do: teaching.

The curriculum

A handful of things, taught by the people who run them in production, covered with a breadth and honesty you will find little better elsewhere:

The full stack of infrastructure

Data, analytics, models, and every glue layer in between, taught as one continuous stack because that is how they fail together in production.

Context, delivered at latency

Getting context in front of a model fast means data systems hydrated with fresh data: schemas, freshness contracts, feature and retrieval paths that don't go stale between two sentences. Most AI applications die quietly here, and few curricula touch it.

Intelligence across the compute continuum

Placing the intelligence layer where the compute actually is, from edge to cloud, latency against cost, and reasoning about that trade-off from first principles rather than defaulting to whatever the last demo used.

The part that matters most

Nothing of real relevance can be taught.
It's absorbed, by watching, by those who discern.

The judgment to know why a pipeline broke at 3 a.m., the instinct for which layer to check first, the taste for a design that won't collapse in a year, none of it fits in a syllabus. It rubs off on learners who pay attention, from people doing the work. That's why our programmes are built around proximity to practice rather than delivery of content.

Teaching it is also how we learn. Explaining a system until it's simple, with nothing hidden, is the strictest test of whether we truly understand it. As Feynman put it: what I cannot create, I do not understand. Every cohort makes our engineers better. We're not being noble, we're being honest about the exchange.

What you actually get

Judgment can't be taught, but you can be placed where it rubs off. Five things we provide, concretely:

GPUs & real infrastructure

You work on the same gear we run production on, GPUs, clusters and all. No browser-simulated sandboxes, no "imagine a cluster."

Mentorship with a seat

Dedicated, booked time with our engineers, at the office, in the room where the work happens. Not "reach out sometime" mentorship.

Shadowing & reverse shadowing

Watch an engineer debug something real, live. Then flip: they watch you drive and coach your hands, not just your answers.

Proprietary certification training

Preparation for industry certifications (Databricks, Kubernetes and the like) taught our way, with access to material you won't find in the public prep guides.

Open office hours, and a seat at the office

Drop into open office hours when you're stuck, and take a seat at our office while you're here. Being in the room is most of the method.

What to expect

  • Cohorts run like an engineering team, which means no sales team calling you about "upcoming batches." When a cohort is full, it's full.
  • You learn incident response by being on call for real ones, alongside the people who own them. Last year's recordings teach history, not the job.
  • The work you do here is open-source projects and large-scale customer simulations, run at production standards. Internships included: real engineering, never billable client work.
  • We show you the routes our graduates actually took, with names and destinations. Nobody can honestly promise you a job, least of all us, so we don't.

Fundamentally non-profit in spirit: we'd teach even if no course ever broke even, because the alternative is hiring from a talent pool that never got shown the work.
