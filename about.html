Who We Are

We're not a training center.

We're an infrastructure company that teaches — a unit of Platformatory Labs. That one difference explains almost everything else about how we work.

1

It started with a hiring problem

Platformatory builds real-time data platforms, stream processors and the Kubernetes plumbing underneath them. So when we hired, we read hundreds of CVs full of certifications and watched otherwise brilliant engineers freeze in front of a stuck consumer group.

They'd consumed hundreds of hours of courseware. They had never once been on call for it.

2

So we taught the way we engineer

The first cohort was twelve people in a room with our own engineers — no slides, real clusters, code reviewed like it was going to production, because functionally it was. The formula was simple: sit next to people doing the real work until you can do it too.

That's now twelve-plus cohorts and ninety-plus engineers deep — many of whom work at Platformatory, and whose graduates now mentor the next cohort. The loop closes on purpose.

3

The part we'd do for free anyway

Teaching isn't a revenue line for us — it's closer to a craft obligation. Training fees cover operations; the GPUs, infrastructure and engineer-hours come from the company's own stack. We teach partly because we need the next generation of engineers to be someone we'd actually hire.

Enrolment and day-to-day operations are run with Acyra, our coordinator & training operations partner — which keeps our engineers doing what only they can do: teaching.

The part that matters most

Nothing of real relevance can be taught.
It's absorbed — by watching, by those who discern.

The judgment to know why a pipeline broke at 3 a.m., the instinct for which layer to check first, the taste for a design that won't collapse in a year — none of it fits in a syllabus. It rubs off on learners who pay attention, from people doing the work. That's why our programmes are built around proximity to practice, not delivery of content.

And teaching it? That's how we learn too. Explaining a system until it's simple, with nothing hidden, is the strictest test of whether we truly understand it. Feynman knew. Every cohort makes our engineers better — we're not being noble, we're being honest about the exchange.

What you actually get

You can't be taught judgment — but you can be put where it rubs off. Four concrete things no training center can offer:

GPUs & real infrastructure

You work on the same gear we run production on — GPUs, clusters and all. No browser-simulated sandboxes, no "imagine a cluster."

Mentorship with a seat

Dedicated, booked time with our engineers — at the office, in the room where the work happens. Not "reach out sometime" mentorship.

Shadowing & reverse shadowing

Watch an engineer debug something real, live. Then flip: they watch you drive and coach your hands, not just your answers.

A guided path, not a gate

Certifications and internships planned into your programme — with a human who's been through it steering, instead of a forum thread and hope.

What we're not

  • Not a training center — there's no sales team calling you about "upcoming batches."
  • Not a video library. Recordings of last year's production incident aren't how you learn incident response.
  • Not a placement agency. No one can promise you a job — anyone selling job guarantees is a charlatan. We show you the routes our graduates actually took and say so plainly.

Fundamentally non-profit in spirit: we'd teach even if no course ever broke even — because the alternative is hiring from a talent pool that never got shown the work.
